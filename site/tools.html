What the WIS means

The Weather Intensity Score adds up every active National Weather Service alert in the country, weighted by how dangerous it is. A tornado emergency counts for far more than a frost advisory. Watches, warnings and advisories all count, and the score is capped so one giant outbreak doesn't blow the scale.

ScoreLabelWhat it feels like
0–59Quiet / MonitoringNormal day. Advisories and the odd flood warning.
60–99ActiveSevere storms or flooding somewhere, watches up.
100–199SevereTornado and severe warnings stacking up. WeBot starts talking on his own at 100.
200+ExtremeOutbreak. Tornado emergencies or PDS warnings. Pay attention.

Watch vs. warning

Watch: the ingredients are there. Be ready, check your plan, charge your phone.

Warning: it's happening or about to. Take cover now.

WeBot's version: a watch is the groceries on the counter; a warning is supper on the table.

🌪️ Tornado

  • Lowest floor, interior room, away from windows. Basement or storm shelter is best.
  • Put on a helmet (yes, really) and sturdy shoes.
  • Mobile homes and cars aren't safe. Leave early for a sturdy building.
  • Keep a weather radio and turn your phone alerts up at night.

🌊 Flooding

  • Turn around, don't drown. Six inches of moving water can knock you down; a foot floats a car.
  • Most flood deaths happen in vehicles, often at night.
  • Get to higher ground early. Don't wait for the water.

⚡ Lightning & heat

  • When thunder roars, go indoors. Wait 30 minutes after the last rumble.
  • Heat index over 100: water, shade, and check on older folks and pets.
  • Never leave kids or pets in a parked car.
